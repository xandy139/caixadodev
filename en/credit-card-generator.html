Understanding the Luhn Algorithm (Mod 10) & ISO/IEC 7812

Created by IBM computer scientist Hans Peter Luhn in 1954, the Luhn Algorithm (also known as the Modulo 10 formula) is a widely adopted checksum algorithm designed to validate a variety of identification numbers, such as credit card numbers, IMEI devices, and national identifiers. Rather than providing cryptographic secrecy, its primary objective is to catch accidental human typing errors such as single-digit mistypes or adjacent digit transpositions.

In modern web engineering and payment architectures, client-side Luhn verification executes instantly in the browser. This provides real-time feedback to end users and prevents costly round-trip API requests to payment processing gateways for syntactically invalid input.

How the Luhn Algorithm Works Step-by-Step

  1. Starting from the rightmost check digit and moving left, double the value of every second digit.
  2. If doubling a digit yields a two-digit number (e.g. 7 * 2 = 14), sum the individual digits together (1 + 4 = 5) or subtract 9.
  3. Sum all processed digits together.
  4. If the total modulo 10 equals 0 (i.e. the total ends in 0), the number is mathematically valid according to the Luhn specification.
