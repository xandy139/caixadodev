How In-Browser Microphone Testing & FFT Spectrum Analysis Work

The Caixa do Dev Microphone Tester processes your raw audio input stream locally at 60 frames per second using the W3C Web Audio API and a 2048-point Fast Fourier Transform (AnalyserNode). This allows you to inspect both frequency energy distribution across the human vocal spectrum and time-domain oscilloscope waveforms, measure decibels relative to full scale (dBFS), and record a 5-second voice sample in RAM to verify acoustic clarity.

1. How to Read Volume Levels and dBFS Telemetry

  • Optimal Speaking Range (-18 dBFS to -6 dBFS / 25% to 75%): Your voice will sound clear, full-bodied, and natural across Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Discord, and OBS Studio.
  • Signal Too Low (Below -35 dBFS / < 10%): Check if your headset boom arm is positioned too far from your mouth, or if your operating system's input gain slider is set below 50%.
  • Digital Clipping / Peak Overload (Above 90% / 0 dBFS): When the audio waveform hits the 0 dBFS digital ceiling, harsh metallic distortion occurs. Lower your microphone preamp gain or enable the Auto Gain DSP filter.

2. Why Use the 5-Second Voice Recording Instead of Live Monitoring?

Enabling real-time speaker passthrough while using open desktop speakers creates an immediate acoustic feedback loop (a loud, high-pitched squeal). Our Record Voice Test (5s) feature captures your speech into temporary browser memory (MediaRecorder Blob) and plays it back cleanly once recording stops. This lets you accurately detect background fan hum, electrical static, plosives (p-pops), or muffled frequency response.

3. Understanding WebRTC Audio DSP Filters

  • Noise Suppression: Attenuates steady background noises such as air conditioning, mechanical keyboards, and PC fans.
  • Echo Cancellation: Prevents audio coming from your speakers from being re-transmitted back into the call.
  • Auto Gain Control (AGC): Dynamically normalizes your microphone input volume when you move closer to or further from the capsule. Uncheck all three DSP filters if you want to inspect the raw, uncompressed signal of a studio condenser microphone.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is my microphone audio uploaded or stored on any external server?+
No! Both the 60 FPS FFT spectrum analysis and the temporary 5-second voice recording happen 100% locally inside your browser's volatile RAM. Closing or refreshing the tab immediately purges the audio buffer.
My microphone is plugged in, but the spectrum bar isn't moving. How do I fix it?+
1) Check for a physical hardware mute switch on your headset cable or boom arm; 2) Ensure the correct input device is selected in the Audio Input Device dropdown above; 3) Verify in Windows Sound Settings or macOS System Settings that your input volume slider isn't muted.
What is the difference between FFT Frequency Spectrum and Oscilloscope Waveform?+
The FFT Frequency Spectrum breaks your voice down into bass, midrange, and treble frequency bars (Hz), making it easy to spot low-end hum or sibilance. The Oscilloscope Waveform plots raw acoustic amplitude over time, ideal for spotting clipping and phase symmetry.
Can I test Bluetooth earbuds (AirPods, Sony, Galaxy Buds) with this tool?+
Yes! Simply connect your Bluetooth headset, select it from the Audio Input Device dropdown, and check the Sample Rate row in the telemetry panel (Bluetooth Hands-Free Profile typically negotiates 16,000 Hz or 24,000 Hz mono).
